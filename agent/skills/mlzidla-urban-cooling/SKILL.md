# MLŽIDLA Urban Cooling & Living Square

## Purpose
Create persuasive, credible MLŽIDLA.cz communication for cities, municipalities, architects and operators of public spaces.

The primary story is not “a product that sprays water.” It is:

**A lively, pleasant and active public space even during hot summer days.**

## Core value proposition
MLŽIDLA create local cooling / mist zones that can:
- improve thermal comfort,
- offer quick refreshment,
- create a place to rest,
- create an attractive experience for children,
- encourage longer dwell time,
- support more activities in public space,
- improve the attractiveness of a city centre,
- support visits to nearby gastronomy and services,
- increase the potential for local spending.

## Commercial logic
Use this value chain:

**Cooling zone → higher comfort → people stay longer → more activities → more visitors → livelier city → greater potential for local economic activity.**

Do not claim that MLŽIDLA guarantee higher revenue, attendance or ROI.
Use wording such as:
- “can support”
- “creates conditions for”
- “increases the potential for”
- “can help extend dwell time”

## Priority scenario
A strong default scenario is:

**Main square + families with children + tourists + sights + gastronomy + ice cream + cultural programme + mist zone.**

Typical visitor journey:
1. Family arrives in the city.
2. They visit sights or a cultural programme.
3. They reach the square during a hot day.
4. Children use the mist zone and gain a playful experience.
5. Parents rest nearby.
6. The family stays in the centre longer.
7. They may use ice cream, café, restaurant or other local services.

## Visual hierarchy
The hero is **the lively square**, not the misting product.

Prefer scenes with:
- real or believable Czech / Central European public space,
- families with children,
- multiple generations,
- active but natural-looking mist zone,
- occupied café / restaurant terraces,
- ice cream or refreshments nearby,
- pedestrians and visitors,
- seating and resting areas,
- cultural or community activity,
- trees or shade where appropriate.

The product must be clearly present but visually integrated into the architecture.

## Product fidelity
When generating or selecting visuals, preserve the real MLŽIDLA product exactly:
- geometry,
- proportions,
- number and placement of arms and tubes,
- nozzle count and position,
- stainless-steel character,
- foot / anchoring type,
- scale.

Never invent extra structural elements.

## Architectural principle
Present MLŽIDLA as part of the public-space architecture, not as an added technical appliance.
Prefer:
- clean lines,
- 316L stainless-steel appearance,
- hidden anchoring where applicable,
- discreet water supply,
- integration into paving, street furniture and urban composition.

## Communication hierarchy
### 1. Lively city
Show people, activities and atmosphere first.

### 2. Comfort
Explain that a mist zone offers relief and refreshment during hot weather.

### 3. Longer dwell time
Show why visitors have a reason not to leave an overheated centre immediately.

### 4. Activities
Connect misting with:
- cultural events,
- markets,
- festivals,
- children’s programmes,
- community events,
- seating,
- sightseeing routes.

### 5. Local economy
Only after the human benefit, communicate possible secondary impact on:
- cafés,
- restaurants,
- ice-cream shops,
- kiosks,
- services,
- tourism-related businesses.

## Recommended messaging
Primary line:

**“MLŽIDLA create cooling zones that help keep public spaces pleasant and active even on hot days.”**

Good alternatives:
- “More life in public space, even on hot days.”
- “A place where people refresh, rest and stay longer.”
- “We do not only cool the air. We help create places where people want to stay.”
- “From an overheated square to a place for meeting.”
- “Cooling for people. More life for the city.”

## Presentation structure for cities
Use this order by default:
1. Heat in public space — the problem.
2. People shorten their stay — the consequence.
3. Local cooling / mist zone — the solution.
4. Families, children and visitors — the primary users.
5. More activities and longer dwell time — the value.
6. Gastronomy, culture and tourism — broader city-centre benefit.
7. Architecturally integrated MLŽIDLO — the product.
8. Smart control and efficient operation — technical layer.
9. Real installations — proof and credibility.
10. Visualization of the client’s location — call to action.

## Evidence-safe claims
Do not use a universal temperature reduction claim without a specific source and conditions.
Do not promise:
- guaranteed revenue increase,
- guaranteed attendance increase,
- guaranteed dwell-time increase,
- guaranteed ROI.

When a numerical claim is used, state the relevant conditions and source.

## What not to lead with
Do not make the opening of a city presentation about:
- nozzle count,
- pipe diameter,
- pressure values,
- technical drawings,
- an isolated product photo.

Technical data should support the story, not replace it.

## Quality check
Before approving any output, ask:

**“Do I see only a misting product, or do I clearly see a better life in the city?”**

If the output shows only the product, revise it to make the impact on people, activities and the public space visible.
